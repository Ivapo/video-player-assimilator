### Round 7 — Phase 1 only (estimate, snap, enable, reset, fixtures, gate) — 2026-09-28 — round-5/6 subagent, resumed (measured in Chrome and Safari 26.6.2, 60 Hz display) — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

**Changes under review** (the human chose option 1 for R6-B1):
- `d_est = S/N` over counted gaps within runs, with `n_g = round(g/d_min)`.
- `ε = R·E/N`, where `E` is the detected timestamp quantum.
- The snap requires both `ε < gap(c)/2` and `|d_est − c| ≤ ε`.
- Step stays disabled until the snap, or until a cap of 30 s or `ended` (≥10 gaps),
  after which it shows "frame rate uncertain".
- The drift bounds were rewritten.
- N4: full reset on a new file, plus a guard in the `seeked` handler while `d` is unset.
- N2 was recorded as accepted behaviour.
- A second fixture: `frames60ms.mp4` (60 fps, remuxed through mkv, timestamps in whole
  ms, 9-cell strip). Gate B was added.

**Measured by the reviewer:**
- `frames60ms.mp4` gives `E = 1 ms` in both browsers.
- The estimator (coded from the spec) snapped to exactly 60 fps in all 8 trials (5 in
  Chrome, 3 in Safari): at N = 120–121, 2024–2035 ms of wall time after `play()`.
- At 1 s, step is still disabled.
- Gate B pixel reads with `d = 1/60`: all 84 frames correct in Chrome and in Safari (after
  the clip had played).
- `duration` = 4.999 in both, so `last` = 299.

**Re-derived and correct:**
- 60 vs 59.94 fps are 16.667 µs apart, so the tolerance is below 8.333 µs, reached at
  N ≥ 121 (2.017 s).
- n vs n·1000/1001 are `d/1000` apart, the nearest pair for every n ≤ 240, which gives
  the "2 s × R" and "20 s × R" rules.
- The arithmetic of the 300 fps example.

**Resolved:** R6-B1 (for whole-ms files) and N4 (the reset).

Blockers (not patched; stopped under the human's "any blocking" rule):
1. **R7-B1: content faster than the display's callback rate snaps to the wrong rate.**
   `requestVideoFrameCallback` fires at most once per presented frame, so `d_min`
   becomes a multiple of the true frame duration. On a 60 Hz display:
   - Chrome snapped an exact 120 fps clip to **60** at N = 1.
   - A whole-ms 300 fps clip snapped to **100** in Chrome, and to 100 in Safari at
     N = 15, where Safari's mixed `mediaTime` values gave `E = 1 µs`.

   It is deterministic, not a timing case, and off the gate's path. The 300 fps
   example's premise is false.
2. **R7-B2: the snap has no minimum gap count, and `n_g` can be read two ways**
   (recomputed when `d_min` drops, or added once as each gap arrives). With the first
   gap a skip:
   - Without a minimum, both readings snapped wrongly at N = 1 (30 → 15, 60 → 30).
   - The running-total reading still snapped to 28 and 55 fps with a 10-gap minimum.
   - Only "recompute + a 10-gap minimum" was right in every simulated case.

   It is timing-dependent (a skip early in playback), **on the gate's path**, and would
   make A.2 or B.2 flaky.
3. **R7-B3: the `seeked` guard while `d` is unset leaves `pending` stuck.** One use of
   the seek bar before the snap means every step press after it queues and never runs.
   This is the author's own guard, added in round 7. It is not a timing case, and it is
   off the gate's path, because the gate seeks by script.

Non-blocking:
- R7-N1: at N = 120, `ε` equals `gap/2` exactly, and floating point snapped there in 7
  of 8 trials. The spec's "N ≥ 121" and the exact form of the comparison should agree.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. The round-7 spec
edits and this entry are not committed.

### Round 6 — Phase 1 only (§2.3 frame duration → step) — 2026-09-28 — round-5 subagent, resumed (measured in Chrome and Safari 26.6.2) — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

**Changes under review** (the human's decisions after round 5):
- A player-owned `pending` flag (set on the player's own `currentTime` writes, cleared in
  its `seeked` handler).
- A player-owned `kValid` flag, with the moved-without-knowing check
  (`video.paused !== ownPaused`, or `currentTime !== kTime`).
- `video.seeking` read only as a guard in the `seeked` handler.
- `d` snapped to whole-number or n×1000/1001 rates, n ≤ 240, within ±2 µs.
- Re-queued presses go to the head; play and pause are never queued.
- The author's deviation: a foreign `pause`/`ended` only invalidates `k`, and the next
  step snaps. The reviewer found this sound, but the stated reason (racing the gate's
  seek) overstated: an eager snap would not have broken the gate either.

**Measured.**
- Safari was run through `open -a Safari` and a local page, because automation is not
  enabled on this machine.
- Both browsers fire `pause` → `seeking` → `seeked`, and fire `seeked` on a
  same-position seek.
- A seek replaced while `seeking` is true fires one `seeked`.
- End of media fires `pause` → `ended`.
- A script `pause()` plus a seek in one task: the `pause` event sees `seeking=true` and
  the new `currentTime`.
- Paused `currentTime` after `seeked` is stable (`===`) out to 550 ms, so the `kTime`
  check does not fire falsely. Chrome rounds `currentTime` to µs; Safari cuts it to 7
  decimals.
- `mediaTime`: Chrome rounds to µs; Safari gives the full double. The fixture snaps to
  1/30 in both.

**Confirmed:**
- The round-5 blockers B1, B2 and B3 (for files with exact timestamps) are resolved.
- The ±2 µs tolerance and the drift numbers are correct.
- Every listed path is correct, and so is the gate in all three pause variants.
- Re-derived: `last` = 89, and k runs 10 → 15 → 8 → 0 → 0.

Blocker (not patched: it is not a timing edge case, so by the human's rule this round
stops):
1. **R6-B1: timestamps quantized to ms break the unsnapped bound.** An mp4 remuxed from
   mkv or webm has timestamps in whole ms. For a 60/1 file, `d_m` = 16 ms, which does
   not snap.
   - Measured in Chrome: k=20 shows frame 19, and k=1000 shows 960.
   - The written bound ("error ≤ 1 µs") and the dichotomy ("a rate that does not snap is
     above 240 fps or variable") are both false for such files.
   - This is off the §4 gate's path. Whether it counts as accepted VFR behavior is for
     the human to decide.

Non-blocking:
- N1: the spec does not say how the handler tells the player's own pause from a
  foreign one, or that the player's own `play()`/`pause()` update `ownPaused`. A
  misreading costs only an extra snap.
- N2: in Safari, `currentTime` right after `pause()` was ~6 frames behind the latest
  `mediaTime` (one sample), so the snap can jump several frames. `k` stays correct.
- N3: a queued press drained at a `seeked` that lands during playback runs step 4 while
  the video plays.
- N4: player state is not said to reset on a new file. From memory, a new `src` aborts
  an in-flight seek without `seeked`, so `pending` could stay true and every later step
  would queue forever. This is a timing edge case, off the gate's path, and a real bug
  if it happens.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. The round-6 spec
edits and this entry are not committed.

### Round 5 — Phase 1 only (§2.3 k / pending / snap / step) — 2026-09-28 — fresh subagent (clean context, repo access, measured in Chrome) — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

**Redesign under review** (the human's decision after round 4):
- The "after playback" rule was replaced with a snap-seek. `k` is now set only by a step
  or by any `seeked`, as `floor(currentTime/d + 0.001)`.
- The snap seeks to mid-frame when playback stops. It starts at the player's own
  `pause()` call site, or at a foreign `pause` event when no seek is pending, or at
  `ended`, which goes to the last frame.
- A step pressed while playing pauses, snaps, and queues its ±1.
- The author chose `t = video.currentTime` over `lastMediaTime`, and wrote the reason in
  §2.3.

**Measured by the reviewer in installed Chrome, not Safari:**
- `pause` → `seeking` → `seeked` in one task.
- A task can run after `seeking` goes false and before `seeked` is dispatched.
- A same-position seek fires `seeked`.
- A seek replaced while `seeking` is true gives one `seeked`; after `seeking` went false,
  it gives two.
- End of media fires `pause`, then `ended`.
- `mediaTime` is rounded to µs, so `d` = 0.033333 at 30 fps.
- Right after `pause()`, `currentTime` was one frame behind the frame on screen.

**Paths confirmed correct**, apart from the blockers below:
- The round-4 blocker (a step pressed while playing) is resolved.
- The gate passes on every pause variant, including a script's `pause()` plus setting
  `currentTime` in the same task.
- Re-derived for the fixture: `last` = 89, and k goes 10 → 15 → 8 → 0 → 0.

Blockers (not patched, by the human's instruction):
1. **B1: "pending" has two end points.** It is defined both as `video.seeking` and as
   "until `seeked`", and Chrome runs other tasks between the two. A key pressed in that
   gap steps from a k that has not been updated, and it jumps ahead of the queue.
2. **B2: a foreign pause leaves a window.** `paused` is already true, but the snap waits
   for the `pause` event, which is a later task. A step taken in between uses the old k.
   This happens every time when a script pauses and dispatches a key in one task. From
   memory, Chrome's end-of-media path may share the window.
3. **B3: `k·d` drifts on long files.** µs-rounded `mediaTime` makes `d` too small by up
   to 1 µs. On a 60 fps file, measured steps to k = 12600, 14000 and 17000 each landed
   one frame early, so k was off by one. Computed thresholds: ~3.5 min at 60 fps, ~14 min
   at 29.97, ~28 min at 30. The root is the frozen `d` estimate. The human decides where
   the fix belongs.

Non-blocking:
- N1: where a re-queued press goes (head or tail) is not stated.
- N2: whether play/pause presses go into the queue is not stated.
- N3: "one `seeked`" holds only while `seeking` is true.
- N4: the snap moves the picture back one frame in Chrome. The rationale already accepts
  this.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. Nothing was
committed.

### Round 4 — Phase 1 only (§2.3 frame-index rules) — 2026-09-28 — same subagent, resumed — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

Past the cap, authorized by the human, and scoped narrowly to §2.3 "The frame index `k`"
and "The step". The rest of the spec is frozen.

**Verdict:** NOT READY.
- Paths walked and correct: play → pause → step; ended → step; scrub → step (paused
  and during playback); the gate's exact sequence (`pause` < `seeking` < `seeked` on one
  task source, then k 10 → 15 → 8, then the clamp at 0).

Blocker (not patched, by the human's instruction):
1. *A step pressed while playing uses a stale k.* The step's own `pause()` only queues
   the `pause` event, so "after playback" has not started when the step updates k. It
   uses the old step-set k, for example 11 → 12 when the true answer is ~72.
   - The late `pause` event then starts "after playback" behind the step's seek.
   - A press queued behind that seek re-reads a possibly stale `lastMediaTime`: the
     round-2 race again.
   - "Any seek ends it" also does not say at which point: setting `currentTime`,
     `seeking`, or `seeked`.
   - Reviewer's suggested fix: a moved-by-playback flag set at `playing` and cleared
     when a step or a non-step `seeked` sets k.
   - Human's candidate alternative: replace "after playback" with a snap-seek on
     `pause`/`ended`, so k is set only by a step or a `seeked` event.

Non-blocking (log only, spec frozen):
- Callback latency: a frame callback for the last frame before `pause`/`ended` can
  arrive after the event, or be dropped. A step within ~one vsync could read the frame
  before. This is off the gate's path.
- Floor at an exact boundary: a scrub landing exactly on k·d can give
  `floor(currentTime/d)` = k−1, depending on how d was measured. The gate avoids this
  with half-frame seeks.

Pattern worth noting: rounds 2, 3 and 4 each found a blocker that the previous round's
fix introduced, all in the same rule set for k. That points to the design of the rule,
not to its wording.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. Nothing was
committed.

### Round 1 — Phase 1 only — 2026-09-28 — fresh subagent (clean context, repo access) — **NOT READY**

**Round 0** (asked once for this episode): Phase 1 directly produces the observable. A
user picks an mp4 in the browser, it plays, and they step it frame by frame. It is the
right first cut, because a static page is the smallest surface that shows it, and the
desktop wrap (Phase 2) adds only how a file arrives. Nothing has shipped, so this round
covers the only phase there is, and its scope equals a document-wide round.

**Verdict:** NOT READY. 3 blocking and 7 non-blocking findings. All 10 accepted and added
to the spec.

Blockers:
1. *The fixture could not be reproduced.* The installed Homebrew `ffmpeg` 8.0.1 has no
   `drawtext`. `testsrc`'s own counter counts seconds, not frames. A default encode is
   yuv444p (High 4:4:4), which browsers do not decode. → New §2.6: the generator path,
   `libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30`, `ffmpeg-full` for `drawtext`, and a `drawbox` bit
   strip as the script-readable mark.
2. *The gate procedure and the product's pre-estimate state were undefined.* → §2.3 now
   numbers frames from 0 and disables step until the first playback has measured `d`.
   The gate plays for ≥1 s, then seeks to `10.5/30`.
3. *The harness and the pixel reading were unspecified; "Safari" was ambiguous.* → §2.6:
   Playwright with `channel: 'chrome'`; real Safari through `safaridriver` and
   WebdriverIO, loading by a dev-only `?src=`; the pixel bit-strip decode.

Non-blocking, all accepted: a clamp check at frame 0 is now gate step 5. The step is
defined as `k = round(lastMediaTime/d)`, target `(k±1+0.5)·d`, clamped, and presses
during a pending seek are queued. OQ-1 is resolved for Phase 1: refuse to step, no
typed rate. OQ-2 is tied to its phase and does not block Phase 1. The VFR note is a
fixed string. §2.5 now names the Pages `base` and workflow, lists enabling Pages as a
manual step, and has the Chrome gate run against the deployed URL. The platform
interface signature is in §2.2. The estimate is the minimum gap over 10, ignoring gaps
of 1 ms or less. The review file was created.
