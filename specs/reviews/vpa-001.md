### Round 5 — Phase 1 only (§2.3 k / pending / snap / step) — 2026-09-28 — fresh subagent (clean context, repo access, measured in Chrome) — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

**Redesign under review** (the human's decision after round 4):
- The "after playback" rule was replaced with a snap-seek. `k` is now set only by a step
  or by any `seeked`, as `floor(currentTime/d + 0.001)`.
- The snap seeks to mid-frame when playback stops. It starts at the player's own
  `pause()` call site, or at a foreign `pause` event when no seek is pending, or at
  `ended`, which goes to the last frame.
- A step pressed while playing pauses, snaps, and queues its ±1.
- The author chose `t = video.currentTime` over `lastMediaTime`, and wrote the reason in
  §2.3.

**Measured by the reviewer in installed Chrome, not Safari:**
- `pause` → `seeking` → `seeked` in one task.
- A task can run after `seeking` goes false and before `seeked` is dispatched.
- A same-position seek fires `seeked`.
- A seek replaced while `seeking` is true gives one `seeked`; after `seeking` went false,
  it gives two.
- End of media fires `pause`, then `ended`.
- `mediaTime` is rounded to µs, so `d` = 0.033333 at 30 fps.
- Right after `pause()`, `currentTime` was one frame behind the frame on screen.

**Paths confirmed correct**, apart from the blockers below:
- The round-4 blocker (a step pressed while playing) is resolved.
- The gate passes on every pause variant, including a script's `pause()` plus setting
  `currentTime` in the same task.
- Re-derived for the fixture: `last` = 89, and k goes 10 → 15 → 8 → 0 → 0.

Blockers (not patched, by the human's instruction):
1. **B1: "pending" has two end points.** It is defined both as `video.seeking` and as
   "until `seeked`", and Chrome runs other tasks between the two. A key pressed in that
   gap steps from a k that has not been updated, and it jumps ahead of the queue.
2. **B2: a foreign pause leaves a window.** `paused` is already true, but the snap waits
   for the `pause` event, which is a later task. A step taken in between uses the old k.
   This happens every time when a script pauses and dispatches a key in one task. From
   memory, Chrome's end-of-media path may share the window.
3. **B3: `k·d` drifts on long files.** µs-rounded `mediaTime` makes `d` too small by up
   to 1 µs. On a 60 fps file, measured steps to k = 12600, 14000 and 17000 each landed
   one frame early, so k was off by one. Computed thresholds: ~3.5 min at 60 fps, ~14 min
   at 29.97, ~28 min at 30. The root is the frozen `d` estimate. The human decides where
   the fix belongs.

Non-blocking:
- N1: where a re-queued press goes (head or tail) is not stated.
- N2: whether play/pause presses go into the queue is not stated.
- N3: "one `seeked`" holds only while `seeking` is true.
- N4: the snap moves the picture back one frame in Chrome. The rationale already accepts
  this.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. Nothing was
committed.

### Round 4 — Phase 1 only (§2.3 frame-index rules) — 2026-09-28 — same subagent, resumed — **NOT READY — stopped, not patched**

Past the cap, authorized by the human, and scoped narrowly to §2.3 "The frame index `k`"
and "The step". The rest of the spec is frozen.

**Verdict:** NOT READY.
- Paths walked and correct: play → pause → step; ended → step; scrub → step (paused
  and during playback); the gate's exact sequence (`pause` < `seeking` < `seeked` on one
  task source, then k 10 → 15 → 8, then the clamp at 0).

Blocker (not patched, by the human's instruction):
1. *A step pressed while playing uses a stale k.* The step's own `pause()` only queues
   the `pause` event, so "after playback" has not started when the step updates k. It
   uses the old step-set k, for example 11 → 12 when the true answer is ~72.
   - The late `pause` event then starts "after playback" behind the step's seek.
   - A press queued behind that seek re-reads a possibly stale `lastMediaTime`: the
     round-2 race again.
   - "Any seek ends it" also does not say at which point: setting `currentTime`,
     `seeking`, or `seeked`.
   - Reviewer's suggested fix: a moved-by-playback flag set at `playing` and cleared
     when a step or a non-step `seeked` sets k.
   - Human's candidate alternative: replace "after playback" with a snap-seek on
     `pause`/`ended`, so k is set only by a step or a `seeked` event.

Non-blocking (log only, spec frozen):
- Callback latency: a frame callback for the last frame before `pause`/`ended` can
  arrive after the event, or be dropped. A step within ~one vsync could read the frame
  before. This is off the gate's path.
- Floor at an exact boundary: a scrub landing exactly on k·d can give
  `floor(currentTime/d)` = k−1, depending on how d was measured. The gate avoids this
  with half-frame seeks.

Pattern worth noting: rounds 2, 3 and 4 each found a blocker that the previous round's
fix introduced, all in the same rule set for k. That points to the design of the rule,
not to its wording.

Nothing was set: `reviewed` stays `null`, and `status` stays `draft`. Nothing was
committed.

### Round 2 — Phase 1 only — 2026-09-28 — same subagent, resumed — **NOT READY**

Blocker:
1. *`k` came from `lastMediaTime`, and "pending" was undefined.* A frame callback can
   land after `seeked`. So after the gate's seek to frame 10, the first step could read a
   stale value from the end of playback, around frame 30. At the clamp, no new frame is

   pixels after `data-frame` matches and the seek is no longer pending, plus one
   callback or 100 ms.

### Round 1 — Phase 1 only — 2026-09-28 — fresh subagent (clean context, repo access) — **NOT READY**

**Round 0** (asked once for this episode): Phase 1 directly produces the observable. A
user picks an mp4 in the browser, it plays, and they step it frame by frame. It is the
right first cut, because a static page is the smallest surface that shows it, and the
desktop wrap (Phase 2) adds only how a file arrives. Nothing has shipped, so this round
covers the only phase there is, and its scope equals a document-wide round.

**Verdict:** NOT READY. 3 blocking and 7 non-blocking findings. All 10 accepted and added
to the spec.

Blockers:
1. *The fixture could not be reproduced.* The installed Homebrew `ffmpeg` 8.0.1 has no
   `drawtext`. `testsrc`'s own counter counts seconds, not frames. A default encode is
   yuv444p (High 4:4:4), which browsers do not decode. → New §2.6: the generator path,
   `libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30`, `ffmpeg-full` for `drawtext`, and a `drawbox` bit
   strip as the script-readable mark.
2. *The gate procedure and the product's pre-estimate state were undefined.* → §2.3 now
   numbers frames from 0 and disables step until the first playback has measured `d`.
   The gate plays for ≥1 s, then seeks to `10.5/30`.
3. *The harness and the pixel reading were unspecified; "Safari" was ambiguous.* → §2.6:
   Playwright with `channel: 'chrome'`; real Safari through `safaridriver` and
   WebdriverIO, loading by a dev-only `?src=`; the pixel bit-strip decode.

Non-blocking, all accepted: a clamp check at frame 0 is now gate step 5. The step is
defined as `k = round(lastMediaTime/d)`, target `(k±1+0.5)·d`, clamped, and presses
during a pending seek are queued. OQ-1 is resolved for Phase 1: refuse to step, no
typed rate. OQ-2 is tied to its phase and does not block Phase 1. The VFR note is a
fixed string. §2.5 now names the Pages `base` and workflow, lists enabling Pages as a
manual step, and has the Chrome gate run against the deployed URL. The platform
interface signature is in §2.2. The estimate is the minimum gap over 10, ignoring gaps
of 1 ms or less. The review file was created.

Re-measured by the author in this round:

- The 7-bit `drawbox` strip decodes back to its own index on all frames 0–89, with
  zero mismatches.
